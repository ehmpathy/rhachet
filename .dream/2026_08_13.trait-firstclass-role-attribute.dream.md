dream =

## first-class `trait` support for roles — booted first AND last

> **dispatch note:** this dream exists to be turned into a **radio task** (`radio.task.push`) once
> radio is unblocked. it is caught here so the intent is not lost while radio is blocked.

### the wish

give a role a first-class **`trait`** attribute. a trait is the role's *identity/persona* layer —
distinct from its briefs (knowledge) and skills (capabilities). the seaturtle identity
(`rule.im_an.ehmpathy_seaturtle`, the tone/emoji/vibe briefs) is the canonical example: it is not
really a "practice brief", it is *who the actor is*.

### the core mechanic — bookend the boot

traits must be **booted FIRST and LAST** in the boot sequence — a **bookend**:

```
boot =
  ├─ 1. TRAITS   (first — establish identity up front)
  ├─ 2. briefs
  ├─ 3. skills
  └─ 4. TRAITS   (last — re-assert identity so it stays maximally front-of-mind)
```

the double placement is deliberate: booting the trait once at the top means it gets buried under
the volume of briefs/skills that follow. re-asserting it at the very end keeps the identity
**doubly clear and maximally front-of-mind** as the actor begins work — the last thing loaded is the
freshest in context.

### why

- **identity ≠ knowledge.** today the turtle persona rides in as just another brief, indistinguishable
  in weight from a lint rule. a first-class `trait` names it for what it is: the actor's persona.
- **recency + primacy.** the boot is a context window; primacy (first) and recency (last) are the two
  strongest positions. traits deserve both so the persona shapes the thought-gravity throughout.
- **portability.** a trait becomes a declarable, reusable identity that any brain can be enrolled with
  — the same way briefs/skills already are.

### the dominant identity — one voice wins

an actor is enrolled with **many roles**, and each role could **ship its own trait**. an actor with
`ehmpathy/mechanic` (the turtle 🐢) *and* `ghlitch/hardener` (the kitten 🐱) would otherwise carry
two rival personas — a muddled, split identity.

so an actor must be definable with **one dominant identity**: it **wins the bookend**, while the
actor still enrolls every role's briefs + skills. only the dominant identity bookends the boot — the
subordinate traits are suppressed (or demoted out of the bookend).

```
actor =
  roles: [ ehmpathy/mechanic, ghlitch/hardener ]
  <dominant: ehmpathy/mechanic>        # mechanic upfront; its turtle trait wins

boot =
  ├─ 1. TRAIT (dominant identity) 🐢   (first)
  ├─ 2. briefs (all roles)
  ├─ 3. skills (all roles)
  └─ 4. TRAIT (dominant identity) 🐢   (last)
```

so: **ehmpathy turtle stays the voice, even though the ghlitch kitten is around.** the dominant
identity is an actor-level declaration (on the actor in `actors.yml`, or an enroll flag). when only
one enrolled role ships a trait, it wins by default; when two-or-more compete, the actor must name
the dominant one explicitly.

**but what grain gets marked dominant — the role, or the trait?** that fork is an open question
(below).

### open questions (for the task to settle)

- **dominant role vs dominant trait?** what grain does the actor mark as dominant?
  - **dominant role** (e.g. `role.dominant: ehmpathy/mechanic`) — point at a role you already list;
    its trait rides along for free, no separate trait-selection to keep in sync. **mechanic upfront.**
  - **dominant trait** (e.g. `trait.dominant: ehmpathy`) — point at the trait directly, decoupled
    from any one role; lets an actor carry a dominant identity that no enrolled role even ships.
  - the role grain is simpler; the trait grain is more flexible. which wins?
- **where does `trait` live?** a new `traits/` dir under a role (peer of `briefs/` + `skills/`)? or a
  `trait:` key in the role manifest that points at the identity briefs?
- **the boot renderer.** the sessionstart boot hook + the `roles boot` path must learn to emit the
  trait bookend (first + last), not just the flat brief/skill list.
- **migration.** the extant seaturtle identity briefs (`lang.tones/rule.im_an.*`,
  `define.why-seaturtles-love-software`, the emoji/tone rules) should be reclassified as traits.
- **de-dup vs intentional repeat.** the bookend intentionally loads the trait twice — the boot's
  token/stats accounting and any dedup logic must treat this as intentional, not a bug to collapse.
- **scope.** does a trait apply per-role, or can an actor carry a trait independent of any one role?
