## .the cue that fired

a human read this branch's cli stdout at stone 5.5 and caught a defect no review round had — a
flat `boot.md (default): …` census line beside a tidy `🧠 brain dir` treestruct. then: *"why was
this allowed? why wasnt this caught in review?"*

two of the three mechanisms live in **this template**, not in the route that used it.

## .the gap, precisely

`node_modules/rhachet-roles-bhuild/dist/domain.operations/behavior/init/templates/5.5.playtest.guard`

### 1. 🔴 `peer: []`

the guard binds **six self lenses and zero peer lanes**, and its only judge is
`--mechanism approved?`. so at the **last** stone of a route — the one whose declared subject is a
human's byhand walk of the shipped product — the only outside eye is the human's.

⚠️ that is upside down relative to every earlier stone. `5.1.execution` and `5.3.verification` each
bind many peer lanes over code and tests; `5.5.playtest` binds none over the **surface a human
actually touches**. the stone closest to the user has the least outside review.

### 2. the friction clause names the wrong artifact

`has-fixed-all-gaps` asks whether the playtest revealed UX friction, and whether it was fixed. read
beside its peers in the same list — instructions unclear, steps ambiguous, citations absent — every
neighbour item is about the **playtest document**. so the friction item reads as the document's ux
too, and a driver answers *"the instructions are clear"* in perfect good faith while the product's
stdout goes ungraded.

⇒ **the lens that should have caught it was there, and pointed at the wrong artifact.**

## 🔴 .a SECOND instance, same absent lane, same day

the census repair uncovered another defect on the same surface — so the evidence for this dream is
**n=2, not n=1**, and both were invisible to eleven stones of review.

every render of an **actor hash** elided it to 7 chars plus an ellipsis:

| surface | rendered | the harm |
|---|---|---|
| `rhx actor list` | `@a84e73b…` | 🔴 the `@<hash>` address a human is told to re-type, un-pasteable |
| `rhx clone list` | `actor a84e73b…` | the same, on the other view the brief names |
| `rhx init` hooks tree | `a84e73b…: ~1` | the only actor id on the screen, maimed |
| the accrual WARN | ``triage with `rhx clone list @a84e73b` `` | 🔴 **a command it hands a human to RUN** |

`genEnrollmentHash` mints **8 chars**, so that value IS the actor's whole name. the elision hid one
character and spent a glyph on the ellipsis — **identical width, zero copyability.** and
`define.address-sigils` states the contract outright: surfaces that emit an actor for a human to
re-type render a **copyable** hash.

⚠️ the accrual WARN is the sharp one: `rule.require.errors-name-the-fix` demands an error name a
concrete next move, and this one named a command whose address does not exist.

### 🟡 the test DOCUMENTED the defect as intent

two unit tests asserted the elision by name — `then('the tree abbreviates the hash')` — so the
defect was not merely unreviewed; it was **pinned**, under a clamp that stayed green.

🔴 and one fixture is why it read as sensible: `actorHash: 'abcdef0123456789'` — **16 chars, twice a
real hash.** against that fixture an abbreviation looks like it earns its keep. a real-width fixture
would have shown 8 chars in and 8 glyphs out, and the question answers itself.

⇒ **a fixture that misstates the real value's shape can make a defect look correct.** that is a
third, distinct mechanism, and it belongs with the snapshot lesson under `ehmpathy/mechanic`.

## .the shape of the fix

### bind a peer ergonomics lane

add a `peer:` block whose subject is the **shipped surface**, bound to the ergonomist rubric that
already exists for it:

```yaml
  peer:
    - rhx review \
        --rules '.agent/**/role=ergonomist/**/rule.*.md' \
        --paths-with 'src/**/*' \
        --diffs since-main \
        --goal 'grade the surfaces a human touches: cli stdout, errors, help text' \
        --output '$route/.reviews/$stone.peer.ergonomics.md'
```

🔴 the measured reason it matters: `rule.require.treestruct-output` **already existed** under
`ehmpathy/ergonomist` and named this exact defect. no lane on the route was bound to it, so the
rule sat unreached for eleven stones.

🟡 the lane is the template's to add, and the exact bind is the behaver's call — a broad
`role=ergonomist` glob may overflow a large diff, in which case it narrows by `--paths-with` per
subsystem rather than drops.

### sharpen the friction clause

split the friction item out of the document-quality list, and say whose surface it grades:

> did the byhand walk surface friction in the **product** — its stdout, its errors, its prompts,
> its exit codes? not the playtest document's instructions, which the lens above already grades.
> cite the render you read and what a human would think of it.

## .why it is not done in this round

the durable home is **another repo**. a `peer:` block added to this route's own copy of the guard
repairs one route and leaves every future playtest, in every repo, with the identical hole — and a
tree adopts only what is scoped to itself (`rule.always.scope-onetime-lessons-to-the-behavior`).

and the local half fails the moment as well: 5.5 is arrived and halted on human approval after six
self reviews and a full byhand run, so a guard edit re-opens the review ladder at the final gate to
grade a defect already repaired. that call is itemized as **F50**, with the cost it accepts.
