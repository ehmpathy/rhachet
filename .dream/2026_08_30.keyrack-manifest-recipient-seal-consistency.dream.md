dream = verify manifest↔os.secure recipient-seal consistency at the domain layer

## .what

confirm that every path which changes a host manifest's `.recipients` re-keys every
os.secure blob in the same atomic operation — the `rule.require.os-secure-seals-to-host-manifest`
invariant. verify it is upheld CONSISTENTLY, at the domain layer, with no sanity-check
pushed into the persistence access layer.

## .why

the 2026-07-30 brick: a manifest was re-sealed to a new recipient while its os.secure
credentials were left on the old recipient. the next fresh unlock derived K, opened the
manifest, then failed on the first credential (`no identity matched any of the file's
recipients`).

the domain choke point `setKeyrackManifestRecipientsSealed` (under
`withKeyrackHostFilesSnapshot`) exists to prevent exactly this: it re-keys the manifest AND
every os.secure blob together, atomically. today all recipient-change callers route through
it (migrate, recipient set, recipient del); the two `.hosts`-only callers (setKeyrackKeyHost,
delKeyrackKeyHost) never touch `.recipients`, so they are out of scope.

## .the settled architecture (do NOT regress this)

`daoKeyrackHostManifest.set({ upsert })` is a plain, unguarded write — and that is
CORRECT. the dao is `access/` layer. per `rule.require.directional-deps`, access holds NO
business rules. the os.secure↔manifest invariant is DOMAIN knowledge; the persistence
layer must not know about it.

so the answer to "should the dao guard against a changed-recipient upsert?" is NO — a dao
that decrypts the prior manifest to compare recipients on every write would:
- pull domain knowledge into the access layer (directional-deps break)
- add a decrypt-of-prior on every upsert (perf + prompt hazard — recipients live inside
  the encrypted blob)
- ripple a public-contract change to all callers

the r10 peer review asked for a structural dao guard; the r11 arch lens approved the direct
`set` as-is. the direct dao set is what we want.

## .the dream (verify, do not enforce-in-dao)

keep enforcement where it belongs (domain layer), and make its CONSISTENCY provable:

1. a domain-layer test/audit that asserts EVERY recipient-change operation routes through
   `setKeyrackManifestRecipientsSealed` — a caller inventory that fails if a new path writes
   `.recipients` outside the choke point
2. optionally, a lint/arch rule: forbid a direct `daoKeyrackHostManifest.set` whose upsert
   payload changes `.recipients` from anywhere except the sealed choke point (detected at
   the domain layer, by call-site, NOT by a dao runtime check)

the point is a consistency PROOF at the right layer — not a runtime sanity-check smuggled
into persistence access.
