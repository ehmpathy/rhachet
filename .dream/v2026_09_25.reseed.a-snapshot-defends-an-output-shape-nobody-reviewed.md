## .the cue that fired

a human read a cli stdout this route had shipped and asked why one part of it was a tidy
treestruct while a census line beside it was a stray one-liner — of a line that had been under
snapshot coverage, green, for the whole route.

then: *"why was this allowed? why wasnt this caught in review?"*

three mechanisms let it through, and **one of them is general**:

| mechanism | general? |
|---|---|
| the stone's guard bound zero peer reviewers (`peer: []`) | no — a guard defect, route-local |
| the guard's "UX friction" lens read as the playtest DOCUMENT's ux | no — a rubric defect |
| 🔴 **a snapshot froze an unreviewed output shape into a defended contract** | **yes** |

⇒ the third is the one worth a durable rule, and it belongs in the mechanic's test practices,
not here.

## .the gap, precisely

a snapshot's whole value is that it **reddens on change**. that value is symmetric and nobody
says the second half out loud:

> **a snapshot defends whatever shape it first captured, with the same force, whether or not a
> human ever read that shape.**

so the lifecycle of the defect is:

1. an author writes a new user-faced render
2. an author adds `expect(x).toMatchSnapshot()` beside it and runs `--resnap`
3. the shape is now **pinned** — and the pin is what a reviewer reads as *"covered"*
4. every later round is green, so no round ever re-asks whether the shape was any good
5. ⇒ the snapshot has converted *"nobody looked at this"* into *"this is the contract"*

⚠️ **the coverage is real; the review is the part that never happened.** and the two are hard to
tell apart from a diff, because the first `--resnap` commit looks exactly like a deliberate
contract decision.

🔴 the sharpest form: **a snapshot added in the SAME commit as the render it pins reviews naught.**
it asserts that the author's output equals the author's output.

## .the shape of the fix

a `rule.require.*` under `ehmpathy/mechanic` `practices/code.test/`, roughly:

- **the claim** — a snapshot of a **new** user-faced render is not coverage until a human has read
  the captured shape ON ITS OWN, apart from the diff that introduced it
- **the test** — *"if this shape were ugly, wrong, or off-contract, which round would have said
  so?"* you can name the round → covered · you cannot → the snapshot pins a guess
- **the cue rows** — a snapshot added in the same commit as the render · a `--resnap` that
  produced a first capture rather than an update · a snapshot of a surface no ergonomics rubric is
  bound to · a `toMatchSnapshot` with no paired assert (that one is already
  `rule.forbid.failhide`, so cite rather than restate)
- **the repair** — read the captured block as a human would read the terminal, in the PR body or
  beside the test, and say what you checked; then the pin is a decision rather than an accident
- **the bound** — this does NOT weaken snapshot coverage. `rule.require.snapshots.[lesson]` and
  `rule.require.contract-snapshot-exhaustiveness` both stand. what it adds is: a **first** capture
  owes a read, an **update** owes a diff review, and the two are different acts

⇒ its natural peer is `rule.require.treestruct-output` under `ehmpathy/ergonomist` — the rubric
that would have caught this render had any lane been bound to it.

## 🔴 .the peer lesson — a FIXTURE can make a defect look correct

found the same day, on the same branch, and it is a **distinct** mechanism worth its own rule in the
same `practices/code.test/` dir.

every render of an actor hash elided it to 7 chars plus an ellipsis. `genEnrollmentHash` mints
**8**, so the elision hid one character, spent a glyph on the ellipsis, and cost a human the
copyable `@<hash>` address `define.address-sigils` requires. two unit tests asserted that elision
**by name** (`then('the tree abbreviates the hash')`).

🔴 **why it read as reasonable: the fixture was `actorHash: 'abcdef0123456789'` — 16 chars, twice a
real hash.** against a 16-char value an abbreviation plainly earns its keep. against the real 8 it
is 8 glyphs in and 8 glyphs out, and the defect is self-evident.

- **the claim** — a fixture for a **domain value** carries that value's real shape: its width, its
  charset, its bounds. a fixture wider, longer, or freer than the real one tests a world that does
  not exist, and can make a defect look correct
- **the test** — *"could the production value ever look like this fixture?"* no → the fixture is
  fiction, and every judgment it supports is suspect
- **the cue rows** — a hash/id/serial fixture whose width does not match its mint · a fixture that
  exists to make a transform visibly act · `'abc123'`-style filler where the domain has a declared
  shape · a fixture chosen for legibility over fidelity
- **the repair** — mint the fixture the way production mints it, or name the exact real-world value
  it stands for
- **the bound** — a fixture may still be *memorable* (`aaaa1111` over a real sha prefix). the demand
  is on its **shape**, never its entropy

⇒ the two lessons compound: the fixture made the defect look correct, and a snapshot then pinned it.

## .why it is not done in this round

the durable home is **another repo**. a tree adopts only what is scoped to itself
(`rule.always.scope-onetime-lessons-to-the-behavior`), so a copy in
`.agent/repo=.this/role=any/briefs/` here would bury a general test practice in one consumer and
leave every other repo without it.
