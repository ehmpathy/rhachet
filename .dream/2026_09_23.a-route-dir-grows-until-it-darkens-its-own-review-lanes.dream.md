# 🌙 dream: a route dir grows until it darkens its own review lanes

- kind    = reseed
- owner   = `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhrain` (the review engine + the guard schema)
- caught  = 2026-09-23

---

## .the cue that fired

`i013` on this stone returned **8 of 11 lanes dark** — no review at all:

```
✋ prompt exceeds 75% of context window
   ├─ 92.5% of 1048576 tokens
   └─ reduce scope or use --focus pull
```

🔴 **and the round before it was clean.** `i012` approved every lane. what moved between them was not
the code — it was the **route dir**, which had absorbed another round of takens, fulcrum entries, and
a yield section.

⇒ the artifact under review grew past the window that reads it, and **the growth came from the review
process itself.**

---

## .the gap, precisely

a whole-artifact rubric binds `--paths-with` at a glob that includes the route dir. every round then
adds to that dir, by design:

| the round writes | per round, roughly |
|---|---|
| one `.taken.by_self` per rejected lane | up to 11 files |
| one `.given.by_peer` per lane that spoke | up to 11 files, each a full review |
| new fulcrum entries | 1–3 files |
| a yield section | 1 |

🔴 **so the corpus a rubric must read is monotonic in the round count, and every round that finds work
makes the next round harder to read.** the feedback is positive, which is the wrong sign for a loop
meant to converge.

### the checkable evidence, on this route alone

| round | lanes dark | what the guard reported |
|---|---|---|
| `i010` | 1 | a complete prose review with no numeric tally — scored as one blocker |
| `i013` | 🔴 **8** | `92.5% of 1048576 tokens` |

⇒ **twice on one stone**, and the second time it took 8 of 11 rubrics out at once.

🟡 **a dark lane is worse than a rejected one.** a rejection carries an argument a driver can answer;
a dark lane returns terminal, unlocks the next level, and **removes a lens from the drive** — so the
drive proceeds as though a rubric had spoken when none did (`rule.always.diagnose-reviewer-malfunctions`).

---

## .the shape of the fix

the remedy that exists today is **manual and per-round**: `rule.always.rerun-dark-review-lanes-scoped`
tells a driver to narrow the bind in the guard, then let the guard re-run the lane. it works — it is
what produced `i014`'s full slate — and it asks a human-in-the-loop to notice, diagnose, and hand-edit
a guard **every round, forever**, on every stone in every repo.

three rungs, cheapest first:

| rung | the change | what it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | the guard **excludes the route's own review archive by default** — `.reviews/peer/**` is process exhaust, never the deliverable a rubric grades | one default in the guard schema |
| **2** | a lane that overflows **auto-narrows and retries once**, rather than returns dark | a retry path in the review engine |
| **3** | the engine **reports a dark lane as a first-class verdict** with its own glyph and its own remedy line, distinct from a malfunction | a verdict-kind addition |

🔴 **rung 1 is the one that pays most and risks least, and it is a one-line default.** the `.taken` and
`.given` files are the record of *how the artifact was reviewed*; they are not the artifact. a rubric
that grades `rule.forbid.decode-friction` against a prior round's review prose grades the wrong text.

🟡 **and rung 1 alone would likely have prevented both incidents on this route** — the review archive
is the largest and fastest-growth member of the corpus, by a wide margin.

### what must be clamped before any of it moves

- `[caseN]` a guard whose bind matches a route dir with a populated `.reviews/peer/` ⇒ the corpus the
  lane receives **excludes** those files, and the count the given reports drops accordingly
- `[caseN]` a rubric that legitimately grades review conduct — an absorption or convergence rubric —
  can **opt back in** by an explicit `--paths-with`, so the default is a floor rather than a cap
- proved by revert per `rule.require.clamp-edge-cases`: restore the archive to the corpus and the
  first case MUST go red on the file count

---

## .why it is not done in this round

the SAFE/CLEAN test (`rule.always.fix-forward-under-scouts-honor`), answered honestly:

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| is it **SAFE**? | 🔴 **no.** it changes what every reviewer reads, on every stone, in every repo that drives a route. a corpus default is the widest-blast-radius knob the engine has |
| is it **CLEAN**? | 🔴 **no.** it ripples into the guard schema, every extant `.guard` file, the review engine's bind parser, and the acceptance snapshots that pin reviewer stdout |

⇒ **both answers are no, which is the row that earns a dream rather than a fix-forward.** and the
subject is not this repo's: the guard schema and the review engine both live in
`ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhrain`, so this is a **reseed** — a tree adopts only what is scoped to itself.

🟡 **`.dream/` is a local queue, so caught is not dispatched.** the seed still owes a push to the
owner repo.
