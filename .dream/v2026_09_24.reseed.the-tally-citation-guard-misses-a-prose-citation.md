# 🌙 dream: the tally's citation guard misses the one citation shape that actually occurs

- **kind** = `reseed`
- **owner** = `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhrain`
- **caught** = 2026-09-24, at `ehmpathy/rhachet` @ `beav/feat-boot-manifest-and-budget`,
  stone `5.1.execution.from_vision`, round `i025`

## .the cue that fired

a peer lane returned a review whose prose reads *"the mechanism is substantively complete and
well-tested"*, enumerates **zero** items it asks the driver to fix, and closes with two items it
explicitly hands to a human. it was tallied **`5 blockers / 3 nitpicks`** and rejected the stone.

🔴 **the tally is numerically identical to the PRIOR round's** — i024 was also `5 blockers /
3 nitpicks`. that identity is what made it worth a probe rather than a repair.

## .the gap, precisely

`dist/domain.operations/route/guard/review/getReviewCountsViaRegex.js:30`:

```js
const pattern = new RegExp(
  `${input.stem}s?:\\s*(\\d+)|(?<![\\w"'\`])(\\d+)[ \\t]+${input.stem}s?`, 'gi');
```

the left-edge guard `(?<![\w"'`])` exists **for this exact failure class**, and its own docblock
(`:19-29`) says so:

> a review whose ONLY numbers are citations reads as undetected (never a silent zero) — see
> `rule.forbid.failhide` + `contract.reviewer-output`

⇒ **that guarantee did not hold.** the docblock enumerates two citation shapes and the guard covers
both:

| shape | example | guarded? |
|---|---|---|
| glued to an identifier | `r11 blocker` | ✅ — `1` sits after a `\w` |
| wrapped in a quote | `` `"11 blockers"` `` | ✅ — sits after a `"` |
| 🔴 **a prose citation, space-preceded** | `claims all 5 blockers were repaired` | 🔴 **no** — a space is not in `[\w"'`]` |

the third shape is the one a reviewer actually writes, because a review that discusses a prior
round writes English rather than a glued token or a quoted artifact.

### the measurement — the regex replayed on the real input

run verbatim against that lane's stdout (the `tallied` footer and the stderr block stripped, since
the regex's input is the reviewer's stdout rather than the saved artifact):

```
stem = blocker — 2 match(es), LAST one binds
           line 13: "5 blockers"      ← a reconstruction of the PRIOR round's stamp line
  BINDS -> line 16: "5 blockers"      ← "…claims all 5 blockers were repaired…"

stem = nitpick — 1 match(es), LAST one binds
  BINDS -> line 13: "3 nitpicks"      ← the same reconstructed stamp line

verdict on stdout alone = 5 blockers, 3 nitpicks
```

🔴 **both matches that bind are citations of a prior round.** neither is a declaration by that
reviewer about the round it graded.

⚠️ **and the probe has a trap worth a line, because a first draft fell into it.** run the regex over
the **saved `.given` artifact** rather than the stdout and it binds on the artifact's own `tallied`
footer (`├─ 5 blockers`) — the file holds the answer appended to the question, so the probe reports
agreement with itself and proves naught. strip from `├─ stderr` onward before you measure.

## .the shape of the fix

three candidates, cheapest first. 🔴 **none is obviously right, which is why this is a dream rather
than a patch** — the choice belongs to the party that owns the contract.

| # | the fix | what it costs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **widen the guard** to reject a digit whose line also carries a citation cue — `claims`, `rejected,`, `r\d+:`, `last round`, `prior` | a word list is a heuristic, and it will drift against real prose |
| 2 | 🔴 **anchor the declaration instead of a guard on the citation** — require the count to sit on a line that is *only* a count (`^\s*[│├└─\s]*\d+ blockers?\s*$`), which is the tree/prose form `contract.reviewer-output` already prescribes | it narrows what counts as a verdict, so a reviewer that declares mid-sentence reads as undetected — ⇒ **which is the documented correct outcome**, since undetected falls through to the brain tactic rather than to a silent zero |
| 3 | **strip fenced code blocks and blockquotes** before the match | closes the reconstruction at line 13 and **not** the prose citation at line 16, so it repairs the lesser half alone |

🔴 **candidate 2 looks strongest and is stated as a candidate rather than a verdict.** it inverts the
question from *"can I recognize every citation?"* (unbounded, a search nobody wins) to *"do I
recognize a declaration?"* (bounded, and already specified by the output contract). its cost lands
in the right place: an ambiguous review degrades to the probabilistic tactic, which is the cascade's
own design.

⚠️ **whatever is taken owes a clamp with teeth** — a review whose only numbers are citations must
read `{ detected: false }`, and the clamp must go **red** under the unfixed regex
(`rule.require.clamp-edge-cases`). the stdout above is a ready-made fixture.

## 🟡 .the second-order cost, which is the part worth a carry upstream

a mis-tally is not merely a wrong number. **it converts a favorable review into a rejection**, and
the rejection then costs a round of the lane's own budget (this one sits at 14/28). so the defect
feeds itself in the one direction that matters: it spends the very resource a driver needs to
answer it.

🔴 **and it is hardest to see from the inside.** a driver who meets `5 blockers` looks for five
defects, finds the review names none, and is left to choose between *"I misread the review"* and
*"the tally misread the review"* — where the first is the humbler read and the wrong one. the
numeric identity with the prior round is what parts them, and that is available only to a driver
who happens to compare.
