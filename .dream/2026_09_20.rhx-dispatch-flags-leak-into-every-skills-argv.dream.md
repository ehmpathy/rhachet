# 🌙 dream: strip the dispatcher's own flags before a skill sees its argv

- kind: fix
- owner: `ehmpathy/rhachet`
- caught 2026-09-20

## .the cue that fired

`aws.whoami.sh`'s silent-drop default arm was cured to a refusal. its very next invocation — the
skill's own documented usage, `rhx aws.whoami --env test` — refused, and the token it named was
**`--skill`**, a flag the skill never declared and the caller never typed.

## .the gap, precisely

`rhx <skill> <args>` rewrites to `rhachet run --skill <skill> <args>`.
`getRawArgsAfterRun` (`src/contract/cli/invokeRun.ts:21-26`) then takes **each arg after `run`** and
hands it to the skill:

```ts
const runIdx = argv.indexOf('run');
if (runIdx === -1) return [];
return argv.slice(runIdx + 1);
```

⇒ so `--skill <slug>` — and `--repo` / `--role` where a caller disambiguates — land in the skill's
own argv. a skill has no business with them: they address the **dispatcher's** discovery
(`run.executable.lookup.pattern`), never the skill's behavior.

the checkable evidence, measured 2026-09-20:

| the skill | its state |
|---|---|
| `show.bun.deps.sh` | refused correctly, declared no dispatch flags ⇒ `rhx show.bun.deps` had **never run** |
| `show.claude.task.output.sh` | declares `--repo\|--role\|--skill) shift 2` explicitly, with the comment *"rhachet passthrough args - ignore"* |
| `aws.whoami.sh` · `perf.test.sh` | dropped every unknown flag ⇒ the leak was absorbed and invisible |

🔴 **two of four skills paid a tax for the leak, and the other two hid it.** the one author who
declared the flags had clearly met the defect and worked around it per-skill.

## .the shape of the fix

`invokeRun.ts` strips its own recognized options before it builds `rawArgs` — the same act
`getBrainCliPassthroughArgs.ts` already performs for `enroll`, and that file is the pattern to
reuse rather than re-derive (it carries a `VALUE_FLAGS` / `BOOLEAN_FLAGS` split and a measured
incident report in its docblock).

⚠️ **one named collision must be settled first.** `perf.test.sh` declares `--skill` as a flag **of
its own** (it sets `SKILL_NAME`, a legacy backward-compat input). a naive strip of the first
`--skill <value>` pair would take the dispatcher's and leave the caller's, or the reverse, by order
alone. either the strip is position-aware (strip only the pair the dispatcher itself inserted, by
index rather than by name), or `perf.test.sh` renames its flag.

⇒ the position-aware strip is the better half: it needs no rename in any linked role, and it is
honest — the dispatcher knows exactly which tokens it added.

once stripped, the per-skill `--repo|--role|--skill) shift 2` arms become dead and come out. they
are harmless until then (a dispatcher that strips the pair simply never fires the arm).

## .why it is not done in this round

**SAFE — 🔴 no.** it changes the argv contract for **every skill in every linked role** — `ehmpathy`,
`ghlitch`, `bhrain`, `bhrowser`, `bhuild`, `rhachet` — most of which live in `node_modules` and are
not this worktree's to test. a wrong strip changes behavior silently across ~40 skills, and the
silence is this invariant's own hazard.

**CLEAN — 🔴 no.** it ripples into `invokeRun.ts`, needs its own clamp, and carries the named
`perf.test.sh --skill` collision above, which is a real conflict rather than a hypothetical.

⇒ that is the `rule.forbid.deferrals-short-of-a-dedicated-pr` carve-out on its own terms: this
grades the **SIZE** of the change (every skill in the tree, plus a name collision), never its risk.

🟡 and the skill-side cure is complete and correct on its own — it is what this repo's own correct
exemplar already does, so the deferral leaves no gap at the grain a caller touches.
