## .the gap, precisely

bhuild ships the `.behavior` route stones and their `.guard` templates, and no brief or check there
ties a reviewer's level to its grain. so a template may place an enrolled-role reviewer at l1, where
it runs before the cheap rubric lanes have cleaned the artifact.

## .the rule to teach

| grain | how it runs | level |
|---|---|---|
| `rhx review` | one rubric, one bound brain call, one `.given` — cheap, narrow | **l1** |
| enroll | a brain enrolled in a role (`rhx enroll … --roles <role>`, then that role's review) — a whole role's corpus, broad judgment | **l3**, always |

⇒ l1 holds `rhx review` lanes alone. an enroll-grain reviewer never sits at l1 or l2.

why:

- the ladder spends cheap lanes first. an l1 enroll lane pays a role-sized corpus on every round
  that the rubric lanes would have caught first
- an enroll reviewer judges the artifact as a whole, so it should read the version the rubric lanes
  already cleaned
- one grain per level keeps a level's cost legible from its grain

## .the shape of the fix

1. a brief in the role that owns the guard templates (behaver) that states the rule, with the grain
   table as its cue
2. sweep the shipped `.guard` templates: every enroll-grain reviewer moves to l3; the `rhx review`
   lanes stay at l1
3. where the guard schema allows it, a check in `route.guard.upgrade` (or a guard lint) that refuses
   an enroll-grain reviewer below l3
