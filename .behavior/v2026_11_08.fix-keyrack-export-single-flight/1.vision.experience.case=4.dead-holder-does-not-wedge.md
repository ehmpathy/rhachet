# case 4 — a dead holder does not wedge the next caller

**narrative (the human).** a `rhx keyrack unlock` took the export mutex, then the human hit ctrl-c — or
the oom killer did. its key sits on disk with no process behind it. the next caller finds the key held
and waits. the key carries the lease as its native expiry, so the cache evicts it once the lease lapses
(3 min, under the 5 min wait bound). the waiter then wins the key, exports, and returns. no human had to
find and delete a lock file.

**fail safe — fast, loud, needs no human:** the lease is the whole fail-safe. a dead holder alone cannot
make a caller pass its wait bound, since the lease lapses first. the wait bound is a backstop for a queue
of hung exports, and case 9 owns it.

```
given('[case4] a process took the export mutex and was SIGKILLed')
  when('[t0] a caller asks for the profile')
    then('it waits out the lease, exports, and returns the creds')
    then('its wait was at most the lease, under the wait bound')
    then('no lock file was deleted by hand')
```
