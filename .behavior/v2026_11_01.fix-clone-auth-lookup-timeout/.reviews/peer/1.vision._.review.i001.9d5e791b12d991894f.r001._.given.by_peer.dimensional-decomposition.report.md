# blocker.1: concurrency / resource-load axis is missing — a documented real path goes un-walked

**rule**: .agent/repo=bhuild/role=behaver/briefs/practices/behavior.vision/rule.require.dimensional-decomposition.md

**locations**:
- .behavior/v2026_11_01.fix-clone-auth-lookup-timeout/1.vision.experience.dimensions.md:14 (the .dimensions table names only caller uid / connection kind / lookup outcome)
- .behavior/v2026_11_01.fix-clone-auth-lookup-timeout/1.vision.experience.dimensions.md:24 (the 2 × 3 × 6 = 36 product carries no concurrency term)

The dimensions artifact fixes the experience space as `caller uid × connection kind × lookup outcome` (2 × 3 × 6 = 36 cells) and never names a concurrency or box-load axis.

Yet the vision itself documents a real concurrency path: `1.vision.yield.md` ("abandoned scans pile up longer") computes that "on a box where `ss` takes T seconds, about T / 1.25 scans run at once per observed say — ~7 at T = 9s, ~32 at T = 40s. a slow box gets slower from its own lookups." That is a genuine experience — concurrent `clone say` callers whose gate `ss` scans contend with each other, so a lookup that would be fast alone drifts into `slow-short`/`slow-long`/`killed` and a same-user say can be pushed past the 30s wedge or the 60s kill *because of other concurrent callers*. The 36-cell product treats `lookup outcome` as exogenous and never attributes it to concurrent load, so this cell has no verdict.

rule.require.dimensional-decomposition is explicit that concurrency is "the dimension that surfaces the non-narratable critipaths (races, eventual consistency) a happy-path list skips" and that "a real path off a missing axis is a blocker: the space was under-decomposed."

The fix: add a `concurrency` (and/or box-load / resource-state) axis — e.g. `first · concurrent` — take the product, and verdict the concurrent cells (even if deferred via fulcrum F6, they must be itemized on record, not omitted).

**snippet**:
```md
## .the walked product

2 × 3 × 6 = 36 cells. many collapse: for `uid = other` every cell ends in a non-pass, and for the
two probe kinds every lookup ≥ 5s ends at the client's 5s reply bound, before and after alike. the
collapsed groups are verdicted as groups in `1.vision.experience.case=_.md`, each with its why.
```

# nitpick.1: axes are listed but not justified as orthogonal to one another

**rule**: .agent/repo=bhuild/role=behaver/briefs/practices/behavior.vision/rule.require.dimensional-decomposition.md

**locations**:
- .behavior/v2026_11_01.fix-clone-auth-lookup-timeout/1.vision.experience.dimensions.md:20 (client windows "cut the lookup axis at 5s and 30s" — entanglement noted, orthogonality not argued)

rule.require.dimensional-decomposition requires the dimensions artifact to name the axes, "each a small closed value set, each justified as orthogonal to the rest." The artifact names three axes and their positions, but offers no orthogonality justification for any of them — it does not argue that `caller uid` is independent of `connection kind` or that `lookup outcome` is independent of either.

Worse, the one correlation it touches on is confessed rather than resolved: "they cut the lookup axis at 5s and 30s" admits that `lookup outcome` and `connection kind` are entangled by the per-kind client windows (a `probe-in-say`/`get` lookup ≥5s collapses regardless of whether it is `slow-short`, `slow-long`, `killed`, or `hung`). That collapse is the signal to either state the orthogonality argument explicitly or model the client window as the reason the axes multiply without conflict. As written a reviewer cannot tell whether the axes are genuinely orthogonal or merely merged around the entanglement.

Add a short per-axis justification of orthogonality (why each value on one axis does not imply a value on another), especially for `connection kind` vs `lookup outcome`.

**snippet**:
```md
the client windows (5s probe reply, 30s say wedge floor) are fixed bounds, not dimensions: they cut
the lookup axis at 5s and 30s.
```

# nitpick.2: `killed` and `hung` lookup-outcome positions may not be distinct (pending F7), and the `killed` cells assume a resolve that F7 questions

**rule**: .agent/repo=bhuild/role=behaver/briefs/practices/behavior.vision/rule.require.dimensional-decomposition.md

**locations**:
- .behavior/v2026_11_01.fix-clone-auth-lookup-timeout/1.vision.experience.dimensions.md:18 (lookup outcome lists `killed` and `hung` as separate positions)
- .behavior/v2026_11_01.fix-clone-auth-lookup-timeout/1.vision.experience.case=_.md:14 (same × killed verdited as "server denies at 60s")

The `lookup outcome` axis carries both `killed` (`> 60s, exec kill`) and `hung` (`promise never settles`) as separate positions, and the inventory verdits `same × killed` after-fix as "server denies at 60s to a gone peer." But the vision's own open research item F7 says the kill may NOT settle at the bound: the command is `sh -c 'ss -xp | grep …'`, node's timeout signals the `sh` not `ss`/`grep`, and `exec` settles only on `close` — so "a lookup past 60s would end at the 65s gate first." If F7 resolves against the kill settling at 60s, then `killed` collapses into `hung` (both end at the 65s gate) and the `same × killed` cell is mis-walked.

An axis position that may not exist, and whose corresponding cell's verdict depends on the unresolved F7 measurement, is an orthogonality/accuracy gap the decomposition should resolve (or explicitly mark the `killed` row as contingent-until-F7).

**snippet**:
```md
| **lookup outcome** | `fast` (< 5s) · `slow-short` (5s–30s) · `slow-long` (30s–60s) · `killed` (> 60s, exec kill) · `hung` (promise never settles) · `throws-fast` (no fd, not a socket, no pid in `ss`) |
```

# nitpick.3: uid = other cells are verdicted itemized/demoed, not forbidden (nurture-barred)

**rule**: .agent/repo=bhuild/role=behaver/briefs/practices/behavior.vision/rule.require.dimensional-decomposition.md

**locations**:
- .behavior/v2026_11_01.fix-clone-auth-lookup-timeout/1.vision.experience.case=_.md:16 (other × fast verdited "demoed — case=2" instead of forbidden)
- .behavior/v2026_11_01.fix-clone-auth-lookup-timeout/1.vision.experience.case=_.md:18 (other × slow-short verdited "itemized" instead of forbidden)

rule.require.dimensional-decomposition defines `forbidden` as "can't occur by nurture — the actor may attempt it, the behavior must reject it." Every `uid = other` cell is exactly that: the actor may attempt the say, and the gate must reject it. The inventory's `.forbidden` section correctly records the invariant ("no cell with `uid = other` ever reaches a pass"), but the per-cell verdict column labels those cells `itemized`/`demoed — case=2` rather than `forbidden`. That mislabels a nurture-barred cell as an ordinary experience and hides, per cell, that it is a rejection invariant (its rejection may be demoed, but the verdict is still `forbidden`). Reconcile the verdict words with the `.forbidden` invariant so each `uid = other` cell reads `forbidden` (with the case=2 rejection noted as the demo of that forbidden cell's rejection).

**snippet**:
```md
| other | fast | `auth-denied` | `auth-denied` | sharp | critipath | **demoed — case=2** (the fail-closed guarantee) |
```

Either add `actor` to the product or record explicitly why it folds into the existing axes.

**snippet**:

actor: a caller of a live clone socket — a human at `rhx clone say` / `rhx clone get`, or a
machine (cron, peer clone) at the same commands. the server gate runs once per connection on its
