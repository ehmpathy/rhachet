# case 2 — peer-beside-a-live-suite · peer · happy · critipath

## .narrative

a claude seat on a 4-core grove is midway through a review. on the same grove, another crew's
clone starts an acceptance run. the seat never asked for this and cannot see it — it only feels
the box.

before: the suite's tree ran at the same priority as the seat, so the scheduler split the cores
evenly among some forty busy processes. the seat's own tool calls (a `tsc`, a `jest` of its own, a
`git` read) waited in the run queue; replies slowed, its own tests timed out, and a 90s jest
timeout in its suite read as a defect in its code.

after: the suite's tree runs at nice 10 and takes one worker on this box. when the seat wants the
cpu, the scheduler hands it over first; the suite soaks up only what the seat leaves idle. the
seat's pace matches a box with no suite live, near enough that it does not notice.

the aha for a peer: a nice'd suite is a guest that waits its turn. the cap bounds how much it can
take; the priority decides who goes first when both want the same core. both are needed: width
alone still lets the suite's processes tie the seat in the queue; priority alone still lets a wide
suite fill the run queue and the memory.

why critipath: the peers are the reason the wish exists. a fix that capped the suite but still
stalled the seats would have changed the numbers and not the experience.

## .bdd `[tn]` timeline (sketch)

```
given a 4-core grove whose baseline (no suite) is load ≈ 6 and cpu psi ≈ 29%
  and a claude seat mid-task on it
  when [t0] a peer crew starts `rhx test.acceptance.credfree --scope enroll`
    then the suite takes 1 worker (shared-box default, 25% of 4 cores, floor 1)
    and  every process in the suite's tree runs at nice 10
  when [t1] the seat runs its own `rhx git.repo.test --what unit`
    then the seat's run takes about as long as it does with no suite live
    and  the load the grove carries rises by no more than the stated delta over its baseline
  when [t2] the suite ends
    then the grove's load returns to its baseline; no process from the suite remains

given a 12-core laptop, a human at the desktop with a browser and two claude seats
  when [t3] a clone on the same laptop starts a scoped acceptance run
    then the browser scrolls and the seats reply at their usual pace — the suite holds 3 workers at nice 10
  when [t4] one seat runs its own unit tests mid-run
    then they take about as long as with no suite live (inside the stated peer-slowdown bound)
```

> the laptop peer differs from the grove peer in one way: a human feels it in the desktop's
> response, not only in a seat's pace. the contract and the bound are the same.
