# 1.vision.experience.dimensions — the acceptance suite shares the box

the experience space, factored into orthogonal axes. the product is walked in
`1.vision.experience.case=_.md`.

## .the axes

| # | axis | values | why it is its own axis |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **actor** | `runner` (a human or a clone who starts the suite) · `peer` (whoever else shares the box: a claude seat, the desktop, another crew's suite) · `ci` (a dedicated runner, no human) | the runner wants speed; the peer wants its cpu back; ci wants full width and a green gate. three goals that pull apart |
| B | **box** | `ci` (dedicated) · `laptop` (shared, a human at the keys) · `grove` (shared, many seats, no one at the keys) | sets the width the suite may take. laptop and grove are split because a grove's baseline load is already high with no suite live (measured 2026-11-14: 4 cores, load 6.2, cpu psi 29%, no suite) — so a bound that holds on an idle laptop may not read the same on a grove |
| C | **box state at start** | `idle` · `busy` (peers already burn cpu, the suite still gets its share) · `saturated` (peers take more than the niced suite's share; its cases slow past their timeouts) · `dirty` (leftovers of a prior run: fixture dirs, orphan brain stubs) | an idle box should give the suite its full share; a busy box must not be starved further; a dirty box is the residue of the defect this wish closes |
| D | **run end** | `pass` · `fail` (a test reds) · `abort` (ctrl-c, a jest timeout, `--forceExit`, a crash) | leftovers are born at the end of a run; an abort is the end that skips every `afterAll` |
| E | **width ask** | `default` · `explicit` (the runner names a width, e.g. `--maxWorkers 1` or `--maxWorkers 100%`) | the default must be right for the box; an explicit ask must win, on any box |

two actors sit outside the suite's own run, so they are not values of A — each meets one surface
the wish touches, on no particular box:

- **prod human** — runs `rhx enroll` in a terminal; meets the leak's prod twin (case 10)
- **pr author** — must prove the bound before and after (case 12)

two usual axes, checked and folded on purpose:

- **actor = system (unattended)** — no husky hook runs the suite (`.husky/pre-commit` runs checks
  only), and no cron does. the unattended runners are ci (already a value) and a clone at a grove,
  which is a `runner` whose header and leftovers behave the same with no human at the keys. no new
  verdict
- **concurrency of like suites** — two crews' niced, capped suites on one box share at equal
  priority and each hold their cap. to a seat, they are one busier `busy` or `saturated` box; to
  each runner, the other is a peer. the bound's peer-workload measure covers the sum. no new
  verdict beyond C

## .orthogonality notes

- **A × B is partly coupled by nature**: `ci` the actor exists only on a `ci` box, and a `peer`
  exists only on a shared box (a ci runner is dedicated to the suite — no peer is there). those
  cells are marked **impossible** in the inventory, not dropped from the grid. the axes stay
  separate because `runner` spans all three boxes (a human may run the suite on ci by hand via
  a workflow dispatch, or on a laptop, or on a grove)
- **C and D are independent**: a dirty box (C) is what a prior abort (D) leaves; the current run
  may still pass, fail, or abort
- **E is independent of B**: an explicit width is honored on every box; the default differs per box

## .the hidden dimension the grid surfaced

walk `peer × grove × busy` and the bound itself moves: on a grove the cpu psi is already ~29%
with no suite live, so *"psi under 20%"* (the issue's done-when) cannot hold there by any change to
the suite. ⇒ a latent axis, **bound reference**: `absolute` (load ≤ cores, psi < N%) vs `delta`
(the suite adds at most N to the baseline the box held before it began). the vision proposes the
delta form and asks the wisher to confirm (see the yield's open questions).
