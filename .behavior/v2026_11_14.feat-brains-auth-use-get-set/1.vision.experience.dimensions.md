# 1.vision.experience.dimensions

> the axes the `rhx brains auth set | get | use` experience varies over. each a small closed set.
> the product is walked in `1.vision.experience.case=_.md`.

## .the dimensions

| # | dimension | values | why it is its own axis |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **verb** | `set` · `get` · `use` | the three contracts the wish names; each has its own goal |
| D2 | **actor** | `human` (at a real terminal) · `clone` (a robot in a worktree) · `pool` (the #575 rotation, no human) | who calls decides whether a prompt (browser login, confirm) is even possible — the `has-human?` axis |
| D3 | **scope** (`use` only) | `clone` · `tree` · `grove` | the wish names three reach widths; each lands in a different place and has a different blast radius |
| D4 | **credential state** | `fresh` · `stale` (access token expired, refresh token live) · `dead` (refresh revoked or account lapsed) · `absent` (no such account stored) | the state the verb acts on; independent of who calls or which verb |
| D5 | **usage state** (`get`, and the reason to `use`) | `headroom` · `capped` (a window at 100%) · `unreadable` (endpoint refused, rate-limited, offline) | what the read reports back; decides whether a swap is worth it |
| D6 | **concurrency / time** | `solo` · `live-reader` (a clone in the target scope is mid-session) · `racer` (two calls that write one account's token at once — two `get`, two `use`, or a `get` and a `use`) · `retried` (the same call again, after a success or an abort) | surfaces the non-narratable paths a happy list skips |
| D7 | **token holder** | `parked` (the refresh token sits in the keyrack) · `signed-in` (the refresh token sits in the grove file, held by live sessions) | surfaced by the walk: the signed-in account is read, swapped and stowed by different rules than a parked one (case=3, case=7, case=9) |
| D8 | **output** | `tree` (a human reads it) · `json` (a machine parses it) | the human and the #575 pool read one result two ways; a defect can live in one and not the other |
| D9 | **keyrack state** | `ready` · `absent` (no keyrack for the owner) · `locked` (it holds the slot, but is not unlocked) | surfaced at execution: every verb reads or writes the keyrack, and each state needs a different fix (`keyrack init` vs `keyrack unlock`) |
| D10 | **brain cli** (`set` only) | `installed` · `absent` (not on the PATH) | surfaced at execution: `set` spawns the brain cli for the browser login; an absent cli is a host fault, not a caller one |
| D11 | **login outcome** (`set` only) | `complete` · `incomplete` (the cli exits with no login) · `other org` (the email is held under a different org) | surfaced at execution: a human can close the login early, or log in to the same email in a new org |
| D12 | **claude's record** (`use` only) | `fresh` (`.claude.json` names the grove's account) · `stale` (it names another) | surfaced at execution: claude reads its signed-in name from `.claude.json`, so a stale record is repaired even when the grove already holds the target |
| D13 | **arguments** | `valid` · `refused` (no `--scope`, an unknown or unbuilt scope, no `--account`, a brain with no auth adapter) | surfaced at verification: each refusal is a caller fault, exit 2, with the valid choices named |

## .orthogonality notes

- D2 × D1: any actor may call any verb. `set` by `clone` or `pool` is **forbidden** (by nurture: the
  call can be attempted, and the behavior refuses it, because a first login needs a human at a
  browser). the inventory records it as cells, not a merged axis
- D3 applies only to `use`; for `set` and `get` the cell collapses to one value (`n/a`)
- D4 and D5 are independent: a `fresh` credential can be `capped`; a `stale` one can have headroom
- D6 applies to every verb, `get` included: each `get` rotates and writes a token (case=9), so two
  reads of one account race
- D7 is independent of D4: a signed-in account can be fresh or dead; a parked one likewise. it was
  latent under D4 until the walk hit the "never refresh the live session's token" cell

## .the product

3 verbs × 3 actors × (3 scopes for use, 1 otherwise) × 4 credential states × 3 usage states × 4
concurrency states × 2 token holders × 2 outputs — collapsed by the `n/a` notes above. D8 and D13 are
walked in their own tables in the inventory, since each varies independently of every other axis.
D9–D12 are precondition axes: each non-default value is one refusal or one repair, so the inventory
walks each as a row under its verb, beside the default product. the inventory walks the collapsed
product by verb, then marks each cell.
