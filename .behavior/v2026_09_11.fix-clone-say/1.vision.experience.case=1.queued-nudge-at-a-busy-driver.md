# case=1 — a nudge at a busy driver reports `enqueued`, not a malfunction

`unattended × mid-turn × brain-queued × probe-capable` · **feel** happy · **care** critipath
(freq: every daemon nudge, which is the daemon's normal case · cost: a false alarm plus a
suppressed nudge)

⇒ the cell the issue reports. **rank 3= by boundary-density** (2 boundaries: the turn queue, and the
two-read conjunct). coverage-subsumed by case=7, and kept as its own spotlight because a sharp
promise owes a focused fail-safe that a journey would bury.

## .the narrative — the RouteReminder daemon's pov

i am a reminder daemon. my whole job is to nudge a driver who is deep in a long tool chain —
that is *when* a driver needs a nudge, so a busy target is not my edge case, it is my design.

i fire `rhx clone say @:beav-1 --what "you can do it! drive on, fulcrum, and converge"`.

today i get exit 1 and `MalfunctionError: message was written to the clone but did NOT leave
its input buffer — the brain never submitted it`. so i log a delivery failure, and a human
later reads my log and goes to hunt for a broken pty. **the message landed fine.** the driver
read it the moment its turn ended. i cried wolf, and the cost of that cry is that nobody
trusts my log.

post-fix i get exit 0 and `😶🎙️ enqueued for @:beav-1 — mid-turn; lands next`. i log a delivery.
the driver reads it when its turn ends, exactly as before — the one change is that i now
**know** that, so i neither alarm nor re-send on top of a message already in hand.

and the change i care about most: my retry policy can now be correct. a `enqueued` must not be
re-sent; a `buffered` must be verified before any re-send, never re-sent blind. before, one word
covered both, so any policy i wrote was a guess.

## .what makes it happy rather than sharp

no human is alarmed and no work is lost. the actor's own words for the outcome (`enqueued`, "lands
next") match what the brain actually does, so there is no surprise to recover from —
`rule.forbid.surprises`.

🟡 the **t4 non-wait** is the line that keeps it happy. a `say` that blocked until the turn ended
would be correct and useless: a 20-minute tool chain would hang the daemon, and a daemon that
hangs nudges no one.

## .the fail-safe it relies on

`enqueued` is a claim about the future, so it must be earned rather than assumed. it is asserted only
when **all three** reads agree — 🔴 `focus: 'input'` **and** input region `clear` **and** the screen
count risen. a region whose count rose is case=3; neither count risen is `absent`. a `enqueued` on a
single read would be a green lie on the exact surface `define.invariant.clone-say-delivery` forbids
one on.

🔴 **case=6 is the proof that one read is not enough.** a permission prompt that consumes our paste
leaves the input region `clear` too — so a verify that tested the region alone would call an eaten
message `enqueued`.

🔴 **and TWO reads are not enough either, which is why the third conjunct exists.**
`countOnScreen` ranges over the **whole rendered screen**, and a prompt is on that screen — so a
prompt that **echoes** what it receives makes case=6's t2 satisfy both halves of the old conjunct.
⇒ the echo is unmeasured (**Q21**), and `focus: 'input'` is what makes this promise safe whichever
way that falls (the yield's `.the input triple`).

### 🔴 and the conjunct is not enough either, unless both halves are RISES

the conjunct was drafted over **presence** booleans, and a presence test hands this very actor a
false green. i am a daemon; my nudge text is a constant. so:

- an earlier tick of mine landed, and its text renders on screen as a user turn
- this tick's paste is genuinely dropped — case=3's race, or case=6's modal
- the post-bound probe reads line `clear` (the drop left naught) and my needle on screen — **from
  the earlier tick**
- ⇒ `enqueued`, exit 0, and case=7's retry rule then forbids the re-send that would have saved it

⇒ **a lost nudge logged as a delivery**, which is worse than the false alarm this wish set out to
remove: that one cried wolf, this one goes quiet. so both halves compare a pre-write count against a
post-bound one, and the repo's transcript verify already does exactly that
(`getCloneSubmittedCount.ts:15-17`).

### 🔴 and the RISE form carries its OWN bound — it is blind to a transient

a rise compares two instants, so it cannot see a value that appeared **and departed** between them.
⇒ if the brain clears the queued region **before** it writes the transcript entry, a t3 probe that
lands in that gap reads input `clear` · screen **flat** · transcript **flat** ⇒ **`absent`, exit 1,
on a message the brain dispatches at that very moment** — this demo's own promise, inverted.

🟡 **narrow and real**: t2's bound fires once at ~15s, so a turn that ends near that instant puts the
window under the single post-bound sample. ⇒ that order is unmeasured (**Q20**), and the yield's
`.the RISE form has a BOUND` carries the table and the two candidate repairs, neither chosen here —
the window's **width** is what decides whether either is owed.

## 🔴 .the bound on the promise — `enqueued` is HOLDS, never WILL DELIVER

the narrative above closes on the retry policy, and that benefit carries a bound the two reads do
not cover:

- `enqueued` is earned against the state `say` **observed** — the brain holds the message, now
- it is not a guarantee about **dispatch**. the turn the message waits behind can be aborted — a
  ctrl-C, a crash, a `clone prune` — and the brain's queue dies with the process
- ⇒ so the very caller told not to re-send is the only one who would have retried, and a nudge lost
  that way is lost with **no retry anywhere**

**so the verdict's own documentation must state it:** `enqueued` means *the brain holds it*, and a
caller that needs at-least-once delivery verifies later rather than trusts the verdict as terminal.

🟡 **the alternative is worse.** for `say` to close this itself it would have to wait the turn out
and re-check — which t4 forbids for the right reason: a turn's duration is unbounded, and a daemon
that hangs nudges no one. a stated bound beats a hung caller, and beats a silent overclaim outright.
