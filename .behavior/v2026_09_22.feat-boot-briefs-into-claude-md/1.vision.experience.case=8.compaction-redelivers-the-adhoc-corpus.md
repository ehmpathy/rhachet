# case=8 — adhoc × survive, the FAIL-SAFE

> **cells #15, #16, #24** — a compaction passes over a long route, and the adhoc corpus is
> **re-delivered rather than summarized**.
>
> **`case=7` demonstrates the LOSS. this demonstrates the REPAIR.** cell #16 is a critipath, and
> a critipath owes a fail-safe: fast, loud, and it teaches the fix. this case is that obligation,
> discharged.

## .why this case exists

cell #16 grades `criti`: `frequent × costly × silent × no wired fallback` is the critipath profile
exactly. the grade is argued in `case=_`; what it *owes* is here.

**an alterpath may be demoed as a failure. a critipath may not.** `case=7`'s `[t7]` is written
to fail against today's behavior — legitimate for a gap it reports, and illegitimate as the only
demo of a critipath. ⇒ this case supplies the absent half.

## .part A — Kai watches a compaction go by — cells #15 + #16

Kai has been on one route since 9am. the clone is deep in stone `5.1.execution` — the stone whose
briefs govern exactly the code on screen — and Kai has stopped to read its work closely, because
it has been right for an hour.

the context meter ticks over. `/compact` fires. Kai barely registers it; a compaction is ordinary.

**today, that is the moment the hour turns.** Kai's next ask is the same kind as the last five,
and the answer comes back *subtly off* — it reaches for a pattern the stone's briefs forbid. Kai
re-reads it, cannot name what is wrong, asks again, gets the same shape back.

> ⇒ the felt experience is **"did it just get dumber?"** — and there is **naught on screen** that
> would answer. the role briefs survived (it still speaks the house style), so the clone looks
> enrolled and sounds enrolled. what left was the *stone's* corpus: it was conversation, and
> conversation is *"summarized with the rest of the conversation"*. Kai finds it by hand twenty
> minutes later, by a hunch — asks the clone to quote a rule it should have held, and it cannot.

**with the fail-safe wired, Kai's minute goes the other way, and it costs one line.** the
compaction goes by and a line appears directly beneath it:

```
🐢 re-booted after compaction
   ├─ stone    : 5.1.execution
   └─ manifest : 7 briefs, 4,180 tokens
```

Kai reads it in a second and does not think about it again — **which is the point.** the next
answer is the same quality as the one before the boundary, and the felt experience is
**continuity**: naught happened. the twenty minutes Kai would otherwise have paid are the twenty
minutes the fail-safe removes.

**and the line earns its place precisely on the runs where no loss occurred.** it is what turns
an invisible repair into a traceable one: the first time a compaction *does* drop a brief, Kai
knows where to look, because Kai has read that line a hundred times and knows what it promises.
⇒ a fail-safe that speaks only on failure teaches no one what normal looks like.

### what produced that line

the `SessionStart` hook carries `matcher: compact`, so the compaction event re-runs it. the hook
re-boots the manifest of the stone the route is **currently on** — a fact the route already holds
on disk in its passage record, so no state is recovered from the summarized conversation. the
briefs land **whole**, appended fresh after the boundary, and the re-delivery announces itself.

---

## part B — the 2am sweep, where the line is read by no one — cell #24

Dana wired the nightly route sweep six weeks ago and has not opened its log since week two. it
drives 12 behaviors across as many worktrees, unattended, and it has never failed.

tonight, worktree 7 runs long. the route is deep in `5.1.execution`, the conversation crosses the
threshold, and the session compacts at 02:14 — **a moment no human will ever occupy.**

the adhoc corpus for `5.1.execution` is summarized away in that instant, exactly as it is in part
A. what differs is all that follows: **there is no Kai.** the re-boot line prints to a log nobody
opens. the clone's next output is code, and its only reader is a test suite that checks whether it
compiles.

> **so the question this cell asks is not "was a message shown?" — it is "did the repair happen
> at all?"** and the honest answer today is that **every signal Dana has would look identical
> either way.** a clone that silently lost its execution briefs writes plausible code that
> violates four rules; the suite is green; the PR opens; the sweep reports 12/12.

with the fail-safe wired, 02:14 is **a re-boot rather than a loss**, and Dana learns naught —
which is correct. the hook fires on `matcher: compact` **because the event occurred**, never
because someone asked. the manifest is re-read from the passage record on disk, which survived the
compaction because it was never in the conversation.

Dana's feel at 9am: **none at all.** the sweep is green, and it is green for the right reason.

**the sharp line: #24's fail-safe must be a MECHANISM, and #16's may be a message.** part A's
re-boot line is a courtesy — it tells a watcher what happened. part B has no watcher, so the
courtesy is worth naught and **only the unconditional re-run counts.** ⇒ this is why `[t5]` asserts
the re-delivery is automatic and observable **in the passage record** rather than in stdout: a
record a later process can read is the unattended form of "loud".

⚠️ **and the two cells are closed by ONE mechanism precisely because the mechanism ignores
attendance.** a repair that branched on *"is a human present?"* would pass #16 and fail #24 — which
is the D4 lesson stated at its narrowest.

## .the three fail-safe properties, each demonstrated

`rule.require.experience-coverage` asks a critipath's demo to show the boundary fail **safe**.
that is three claims, and this case must carry all three:

| property | how this case shows it |
|---|---|
| **fast** | the repair fires **on the compaction event itself**, not on the next stone, not on the next human prompt. the window in which the clone holds a degraded corpus is zero turns |
| **loud** | the re-boot emits a line that names the stone and manifest. ⚠️ **and "loud" is graded against cell #24's reader-less run** — so the re-delivery must be observable in the passage record, not only in stdout |
| **teaches the fix** | the emitted line names the mechanism (`matcher: compact`) and the manifest path, so a human who sees an unexpected re-boot can find why. a repair nobody can trace is a repair nobody trusts |

🟡 **the third property is the one a re-delivery most easily fails.** a silent automatic repair is
still a silent behavior, and this feature exists to kill silent behaviors — the helpful ones as
well.

## .why ONE mechanism closes both #16 and #24

the compact matcher is **attendance-independent by construction**: it is an event hook, so it
fires whether or not a human is at the terminal. that is unusual in this design — every other
sharp cell's remedy is a printed message, and a printed message has no reader in a fleet.

⇒ **cell #24 is normally strictly harder than its attended twin. here it is not**, and the reason
is worth carry to the blueprint as a preference: **a remedy built as an event is
attendance-proof; a remedy built as a message is not.**

## .the bdd sketch

```
given [case8] a long route session that has booted an adhoc manifest at stone 5.1

  when  [t0] the context fills and /compact fires
  then  the role corpus still applies, unchanged
  ⇒ the baseline: cell #7, already demoed in case=7

  when  [t1] the compaction completes, with NO compact matcher wired
  then  the adhoc corpus is a lossy summary, not the briefs
  and   no signal was raised
  ⇒ the loss. this assertion is the one case=7 [t7] already carries

  when  [t2] the compaction completes WITH matcher: compact wired
  then  the hook re-runs on the compaction event
  and   the manifest re-booted is the one for the stone the route is CURRENTLY on
  and   that stone is read from the route's passage record, never from the summary
  ⇒ the repair must not depend on the context it repairs

  when  [t3] the clone is asked to quote the adhoc payload after [t2]
  then  it reproduces the content verbatim
  ⇒ FAST — the degraded window is zero turns

  when  [t4] the re-delivery occurs
  then  a line is emitted that names the stone and the manifest re-booted
  and   the line names the mechanism, so the cause is traceable
  ⇒ LOUD + TEACHES

  when  [t5] the session is unattended and no one reads stdout       [cell #24]
  then  the re-delivery still occurs, unconditionally
  and   it is observable in the passage record, not only in stdout
  ⇒ the attendance-independence claim, asserted rather than assumed

  when  [t6] the re-booted manifest is byte-identical to the one already in context
  then  the boot is idempotent — the payload is not doubled
  ⇒ the same guard case=5 [t5] asserts, re-checked at a compaction boundary

  when  [t7] two compactions occur in one session
  then  each re-delivers, and the corpus does not accumulate duplicates
  ⇒ the repair must be safe to repeat, since compactions are not rare

  when  [t8] the compact matcher is wired but the manifest file is now absent
  then  the re-delivery FAILS LOUD rather than degrades silently
  ⇒ a fail-safe that fails silently is not a fail-safe. this is the guard on the guard
```

## 🟡 .what this case does NOT settle

- **which remedy the blueprint picks.** the compact matcher is the known lever and the one this
  case demonstrates; it is not declared the only possible one. what is settled is that **a
  remedy is owed** — criteria may not close #16 by argument alone.
- **whether the re-boot should carry the stone's manifest or the whole route's.** `[t2]` asserts
  the current stone; a route that wants the full history would double the payload on every
  compaction. ⇒ stated as the narrower default, and open to the blueprint.
- **the transport was RE-RULED (S12), and this case is untouched — for a reason worth a note.**
  the adhoc corpus was **never** on the transport F1 decides. it is conversation content in layer
  3, and *"summarized with the rest of the conversation"* is a property of the conversation layer,
  not of the role corpus's pipe. ⇒ no re-walk of **#15, #16, #24** is owed on F1's account; their
  verdicts hold under either verdict, and **the fail-safe this case demonstrates stays NECESSARY.**

  ⚠️ **one argument beneath it DID fall, and it was the second of two.** this case read that the
  adhoc corpus keeps the conversation layer partly because *"a recorded system prompt is inert
  mid-session, so an adhoc boot could never have ridden it."* a layer-2 transport is **re-read**,
  so that bar is gone. what remains is the **prefix** argument — a mid-route rewrite of a cached
  prefix invalidates every token after it, and a re-read that lands at an unpredictable moment is
  worse than one that never lands. ⇒ **the conclusion holds on one argument where it had two**
  (`dimensions.md` invariant 2).
