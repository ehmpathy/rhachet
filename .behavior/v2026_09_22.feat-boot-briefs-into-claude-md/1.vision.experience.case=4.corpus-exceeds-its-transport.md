# case=4 — the two size caps, in both attendance positions

> **cells #4 + #10** (attended) and **cells #18 + #21** (unattended), demoed together because they
> are the **same hazard met at two moments** — and the contrast between them is the lesson.
>
> **the unattended half is the sharper half**, and it is the reason the `attendance` axis was
> promoted: #4's only detection channel is *a human notices the preview*, and #10's whole value is
> *a human reads four remedies*. remove the human and both remedies evaporate.

## .the narrative — part A: today's silent truncation (cell #4)

**Mira, who is new to the repo, on her first day.**

she has been told the mechanic role is where the house style lives. she enrolls a clone against it
and watches the boot scroll past — a readme, a tidy `<stats>` block, `tokens ≈ 57,459`. green. she
asks her first question: *"should this take positional args?"*

the clone says positional args are fine here.

she is surprised, because a teammate told her the opposite an hour ago. she scrolls back up through
the boot output, and near the top she finds the line she had skimmed past:

```
<brief.say path=".agent/…/rule.forbid.positional-args.md">
  rule: avoid positional args

  why: hard to read, easy to break, even in bash
  …
  [output truncated — 229,835 chars written to /tmp/claude/hook-…/output.txt]
```

**she was handed a 2,000-character preview of a 229,835-character corpus, and told it was the
corpus.** the rule she needed is on disk, at a path the clone was given and never opened — the
"phantom read". what she got was the first ~1%, which happened to carry a rule's *title* and not
its body.

**and no part of that is an error.** the hook exits 0. the boot reports success. the stats block
cheerfully prints `tokens ≈ 57,459` — **the size of what was RENDERED, never the size of what was
DELIVERED.** the clone behaves like a clone with ~1% of its briefs, and **every signal in the system
says the boot worked.**

> **the feel: gaslit by a green checkmark.** Mira's only route to the truth was to doubt an
> output that said `✅` and go read it character by character. the number printed beside the
> success is the number that lied.

> **this is the worst shape a defect can take: loud success, quiet wrongness.**

## .the narrative — part B: the gate that already exists (cell #10)

**Sam, who authors the next stone's manifest, ten minutes later.**

he is at the same terminal Mira was. he adds a brief set to the stone's `boot.yml` — five entries,
each one obviously worth it — and runs the boot to check his work. he expects a payload.

he gets a halt, and no payload at all:

```
🧢 repo introspect  (role=over.snapshot)
   ├─ ✋ over budget
   │  ├─ budget   = 20 tokens   (declared in boot.yml)
   │  ├─ payload  = 485 tokens   (full emitted payload, o200k_base)
   │  └─ over by  = 465 tokens   (2425% of budget)
   │
   ├─ some say resources must go — you choose which
   │
   └─ fix — four strategies, cheapest first
      ├─ catalogize  replace a set with one catalog that indexes it
      ├─ condense    author an x.md.min beside x.md
      ├─ reference   move a say entry to ref (it stays addressable, costs its path line)
      └─ eliminate   delete what does not earn its boot
                     ⇒ or raise budget.tokens deliberately — it lands on the diff
```

Sam reads it top to bottom. he learns three facts he did not have ten seconds ago: **which** budget
he broke, by **how much**, and **four** ways out ordered cheapest-first. he picks `condense`, writes
an `x.md.min` beside the brief, re-runs, and the boot emits.

**total elapsed: under two minutes. no clone was ever spawned against a partial corpus.**

> **the feel: caught, not blamed.** the halt refuses his work and hands him the cure in the same
> breath — and the escape hatch it offers (*"raise `budget.tokens` deliberately"*) is followed by
> the reason it is safe: **it lands on the diff**, where a reviewer sees it. Sam is not tempted to
> route around the gate, because the gate did not ask him to guess.

**same overflow as part A. opposite experience.** Mira had to doubt a green checkmark and read
229,835 characters to find her defect; Sam was handed his in four lines before it existed.

⇒ **the difference is not the message's quality — it is WHEN the corpus was measured.** part A
measures at delivery, where the only observer is a clone that cannot tell what it did not receive.
part B measures at bind, where a human is holding the file that caused it.

## .the lesson, and why these share one case

| | today (cell #4) | the gate (cell #10) |
|---|---|---|
| **when** | runtime, per session | author time, once |
| **signal** | exit 0, success reported | **halt** |
| **who learns** | nobody | the author, immediately |
| **the remedy** | undiscoverable | four, named, cost-ordered |
| **the escape hatch** | — | explicit, and reviewable |

**and the gate serves the ROLE path too — `[t1a]`.** *"the new transport has no cap"* does not
discharge cell #4: it asserts the edge away rather than demonstrates a fail-safe, and **"no
documented cap" is an absence of evidence.** the changelog caps every other carrier it mentions and
is silent on these flags; silence is the weakest guarantee a transport can offer.

⇒ so the attended half of cell #4 owes the same halt the adhoc path gets — **aimed at whatever
limit the chosen transport turns out to have.** one gate, two numbers.

⇒ **the fix for the adhoc corpus is not a bigger pipe. it is this gate, aimed at the hook cap.**
that is candidate **A** in the yield: set an adhoc manifest's `budget.tokens` to fit under the
10,000-character hook cap, and the extant gate makes the fit **enforceable at author time**
rather than a silent truncation at runtime.

the peer branch built the mechanism. this feature points it at a new number.

## .the bdd sketch

```
given [case4] a corpus larger than its transport can carry

  when [t0] a ROLE corpus exceeds the hook's 10,000-character cap (today's path)
  then    the clone receives a preview, not the corpus
  and     the boot reports success
  ⇒ this is the defect under repair — asserted so the repair is provable

  when [t1] the same role corpus travels the new transport
  then    it is delivered whole
  and     no DOCUMENTED cap applies to it
  ⇒ 🟡 "no documented cap" is an ABSENCE OF EVIDENCE, never a guarantee.
     the changelog caps everything else it mentions — mcp 2,048, hooks 10,000,
     skill descriptions 1,536, MEMORY.md 25KB — and is silent on these flags.
     silence is the weakest thing a transport can offer, so [t1] alone is
     NOT a fail-safe. the fail-safe is [t1a] below

  when [t1a] the role corpus exceeds what the NEW transport can carry
             — an undocumented cap, an ARG_MAX ceiling, a gateway limit
  then    the bind HALTS before the clone is spawned
  and     the halt names the transport's observed limit and the corpus size
  and     it names the same four remedies part B gives the adhoc path
  and     no clone is spawned against a partial corpus
  ⇒ the ATTENDED fail-safe for cell #4. a critipath may not be discharged
     by the claim that its edge was removed — an edge nobody has measured is
     an edge that has not been removed.
     ⇒ the remedy is the one part B already built, aimed at a second number

given [case4b] an ADHOC manifest with a declared token budget

  when [t2] the manifest's payload fits the budget
  then    the boot renders and emits

  when [t3] the manifest's payload EXCEEDS the budget
  then    the boot halts before it emits
  and     the halt names the budget, the payload, and the overage
  and     the halt names four remedies, cheapest first
  and     no partial payload reaches the clone

  when [t4] the budget is raised deliberately in boot.yml
  then    the boot proceeds
  and     the raise is visible in the diff

  when [t5] a manifest declares NO budget
  then    no gate applies
  ⇒ the gate is opt-in per manifest (the peer branch's requirement 4)
```

## .the UNATTENDED half — cells #18 and #21

### cell #18 — a degraded delivery nobody notices

#### .the narrative

a nightly job drives a route end to end. no human is at the terminal; the output goes to a CI log.

the job spawns a clone on the `mechanic` role. the corpus is 229,835 characters. the transport
carries what it can and truncates the rest.

**the clone opens and starts work.** it writes code. the code compiles, the tests pass, the PR
opens. every gate is green.

and every file it touched has gerunds in the identifiers, positional arguments in three
signatures, and a `try/catch` that swallows an error — **because the briefs that forbid all three
were in the 98% that did not arrive.** the clone was never told. it produced confident, plausible
work that breaks house style at every turn, and reported success at every step.

Dana reviews the PR the next day and cannot make sense of it: the clone has been reliable for
months. she re-runs it locally, at her terminal, and it is fine — **her boot was not truncated,
because the same corpus that overflows in CI fits under her different local conditions.**

> **the feel: a clone that got quietly worse, and an engineer who cannot reproduce it.** the
> attended path has a tell — a human glances at a 2,000-character preview and thinks *"that looks
> wrong."* unattended, **there is no glance**, and the defect presents as a personality change with
> no cause anyone can point at.

#### .why the attended remedy evaporates

part A's defect has exactly one detection channel today: **a human sees a 2,000-character preview
where a corpus should be and thinks "that looks wrong."** it is a weak channel, and unattended it
is **absent**.

> **so #18 is strictly worse than #4, and by a mechanism rather than a degree.** the clone boots
> with ~1% of its briefs, reports success, and produces plausible answers that are wrong in exactly
> the dimension the briefs were meant to govern. no human is in the loop to be suspicious.

⇒ **the remedy cannot be presentational.** the boot must *assert* what it delivered:

```
given [case4.u1] an UNATTENDED boot whose corpus exceeds its transport

  when  [t6] the corpus is truncated in transit
  then  the boot DETECTS the truncation rather than reports success
  and   it compares what it rendered against what the transport accepted
  ⇒ a rendered-vs-delivered assertion. the one check that needs no reader

  when  [t7] the delivered corpus is whole
  then  the assertion passes and the boot is silent
  ⇒ the check must be cheap enough to run every boot, so it must not be chatty

  when  [t8] the assertion fails
  then  the boot exits non-zero
  and   the clone is NOT spawned against a degraded corpus
  ⇒ the same fail-fast bound as case=3 [t6]

  when  [t8b] the rendered artifact is ABSENT or UNREADABLE at delivery
              — deleted, never written, or corrupt
  then  the assertion fails LOUD rather than reports a successful boot
  and   the boot exits non-zero, exactly as [t8] does
  ⇒ the SECOND experience in cell #4. delivered=0 against rendered=N is a
     comparison that already fails, so ONE mechanism closes both — but the
     zero case must be WALKED, never merely declared, because it is the case
     with no fallback at all: an absent artifact is otherwise indistinguishable
     from a role with no briefs, and every human-faced signal reports a
     healthy boot
```

🟡 **`[t6]` is the one genuinely new requirement this cell produces**, and it is cheap: the boot
already assembles the payload as a buffer (`genBootPayload`), so it already knows the number it
intended to deliver. **an assertion is a comparison, not a new measurement.**

**and `[t8b]` is why that assertion must compare a NUMBER rather than check a flag.** a
truncation and an absent artifact are the same defect at two magnitudes — a delivery that
under-delivers in silence — so a check written as *"did the transport accept it?"* passes the
absent case while a check written as *"does delivered equal rendered?"* catches both. ⇒ the cell's
two experiences share one remedy **only if the remedy is stated as an equality**, and that is a
contract detail the blueprint owes rather than an implementation liberty.

### cell #21 — a halt with four remedies, and no one to read them

#### .the narrative

the same nightly job, a week later. a stone's manifest has grown — someone added three briefs to
it in a PR that merged clean, because the budget gate is an author-time halt and **the author ran
it at a terminal, where it fit.** the CI host's tokenizer accounting differs by a hair. it does
not fit there.

**the job hits the gate at 03:40.** the gate does its whole job: it names the budget, the payload,
the overage, and four remedies ordered cheapest-first, with the escape hatch and the reason the
escape hatch is safe.

the route driver is a process. it reads none of it.

what happens next depends on a detail nobody specified: **if the halt is a printed block, the
driver proceeds** — it boots the next stone without the briefs the halted one owed, and the route
runs to completion on a corpus it never received. **if the halt is a throw, the job dies at 03:40
with a stack trace and no summary.**

Rae sees a red build. the log's last useful line is the four-remedy block, which tells her exactly
what to do — *if she scrolls to it.* the build status itself says only `exit 1`, the same thing it
says for a flaky network call.

> **the feel: the best error message in the system, delivered to a process.** and the irony is
> sharp — part B exists *because* a silent truncation is worse than a loud halt. unattended, **the
> loud halt is silent too**, and the gate's entire value is the part that does not survive.

#### .why the attended remedy evaporates

part B's gate is **a message by design**. its value is the four cost-ordered remedies and the
reviewable escape hatch — all of it written for a human to read and act on.

an unattended route driver that hits that halt gets a wall of prose it cannot act on. **and worse:
if the halt is only a printed block, the run may continue.**

```
given [case4.u2] an UNATTENDED route driver whose adhoc manifest exceeds its budget

  when  [t9] the budget gate halts
  then  the process exits NON-ZERO
  and   the exit distinguishes "over budget" (a constraint) from an internal fault
  ⇒ a CI run fails the build rather than stalls on unread prose

  when  [t10] the halt is emitted
  then  the overage is present in a machine-readable form — budget, payload, over-by
  and   the human-readable four-remedy block is ALSO emitted
  ⇒ additive, never a substitute. the operator who reads the log later still gets the lesson

  when  [t11] a fleet sweep boots N manifests and one exceeds its budget
  then  that one manifest is individually identifiable from the machine-readable output
  ⇒ a sweep that reports "1 of N failed" and omits WHICH is a sweep that hides
```

**the shared conclusion of #18 and #21, and of #17 in `case=3`:** this design's remedies were
all authored as *messages*, and the fleet the wish is framed around has no reader. ⇒ **each sharp
cell owes a machine-detectable form of its signal** — a criteria requirement, not an open question.

## 🟡 .the verification this case owes

the budget that protects the adhoc path must be set **below the hook cap**. that requires one fact
this round did not settle:

> **is the 10,000-character cap applied per hook invocation, or to the aggregate of all hooks on the event?**

- **per invocation** ⇒ N chunked hooks multiply the cap, and the budget can be generous
- **aggregate** ⇒ the budget must account for every other hook on `SessionStart` too, which makes
  it a *shared* resource and considerably tighter

⇒ carried to criteria as an open question. **it is not assumed in either direction.**
