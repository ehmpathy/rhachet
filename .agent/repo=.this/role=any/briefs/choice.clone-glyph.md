# choice.clone-glyph

> **scope — `repo=.this` (this rhachet repo only).** the etymology behind one glyph in this repo's
> emoji language (`rule.prefer.emoji-language`): why the **clone** domain-root is `😶`, a mouthless
> face.

## .what

the clone domain-root glyph is `😶` — a face with no mouth. this brief holds the etymology.

`😶` for the clone, `🎭` for the actor. an **actor** is the identity — the recipe, the roles, the
performer (the expressive masks). a **clone** is a different grain: a live, reachable instance spawned
from that identity. two grains, two glyphs.

## .why a mouthless face

- **its voice comes from its roles.** a clone has **no voice of its own** — it is a blank, mouthless
  face until the actor's roles give it a mouth to speak with. what it says and does comes entirely
  from the actor's DNA (brain + roles), never from the glyph. `😶` shows exactly that: a face present
  and ready, but silent until animated by the role it wears.
- **an exact replica, no character.** the one invariant the framework knows about a clone is that it
  is an **exact replica of the actor's DNA** (brain + roles). its behavior is arbitrary — whatever
  that DNA dictates — so the glyph must impose **no** temperament of its own. a featureless face does
  exactly that.
- **it never collides with a role-mascot.** a role-mascot is a character with a voice (`🐢`, `🦉`).
  `😶` is deliberately character-less and mouthless, so no role could ever claim
  it as a mascot — the clone root stays cleanly in the neutral domain-root space, forever separate
  from the mascot space. a creature glyph (a sheep, say) could collide the day a role wants that
  creature; a blank face cannot.
- **it pairs with `🎭`.** the actor is the expressive masks — the faces and the voices. the clone is
  the blank face those masks animate. `🎭 actors` → `😶 clones` reads as "the identity, and the
  faceless copies that wear it."

## .the blank face is fidelity, not lack — regard a clone with respect

`😶` is empty of *pretense*, never of *worth*. the face is blank so it can carry the actor's identity
faithfully, without a personality forced on it — that emptiness is fidelity, the way clear glass is
faithful to whatever it frames. a clone is a real worker that renders real work; it deserves the same
care and respect as the actor it carries. read the glyph as dignity, not diminishment: a faithful
instance, held in regard, that speaks with the voice its roles give it.
